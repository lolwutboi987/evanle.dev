Project Overview

The Interactive Energy Map (accessible at evanle.dev/energy-map.html) is a dynamic digital interface designed to visualize regional and global energy distribution, infrastructure, and the environmental impacts of the current energy mix. To maximize engagement, the platform integrates two interactive sub-features: a City Comparison Tool and an Energy Literacy Quiz.

Topic Selection & Justification

The project focuses on comparing fossil fuels against renewable energy sources. This topic was chosen because energy generation represents the single largest contributor to global greenhouse gas emissions. Developing a clear understanding of power sources is a critical first step toward environmental literacy and systemic infrastructure change.

Methodology & Technical Justification

The chosen method is a custom-built, interactive web interface rather than a static presentation or report.

The Problem: Traditional textbooks and spreadsheets of climate data can be dry, overwhelming, and inaccessible to the general public.

The Solution: A dynamic digital map allows users to engage directly with spatial data. By toggling specific data layers, users can immediately observe the geographic relationships between energy plants, resource availability, and environmental footprints.

Target Audience & Accessibility

The primary audience consists of the general public, specifically students, community members, and individuals without a background in environmental science.

Large-scale climate solutions require widespread community buy-in. By translating complex data into an intuitive visual format, the tool allows anyone to understand their regional power grid without needing expertise in environmental chemistry or utility engineering.

Core Features & Walkthrough

1. Geospatial Data Layers

The interface operates through distinct, scientifically validated data layers:

  • The Status Quo Layer: Visualizes where non-renewable sources like coal and natural gas currently dominate, illustrating their proximity to high-emissions zones and vulnerable ecosystems.
  • The Renewable Potential Layer: Highlights geographic areas optimized for clean energy production, such as regions with high solar irradiance, strong wind corridors, or geothermal potential.

2. City Comparison Tool

This feature allows users to select two different municipalities to compare their energy profiles side-by-side. It contrasts their reliance on fossil fuels versus renewables, local carbon output, and proximity to clean energy infrastructure, making global grid data tangible on a local, relatable scale.

3. Interactive Energy Quiz

To reinforce learning, the embedded quiz tests users on energy concepts and regional grid facts. It serves as a gamified checkpoint, turning passive viewers into active participants and measuring their understanding of how energy choices impact the environment.

Scientific Validity & Data Integrity

To ensure absolute scientific soundness, all spatial and statistical data is aggregated from official, peer-reviewed repositories, including:

  • The U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA)
  • The National Renewable Energy Laboratory (NREL)
  • The EPA Power Profiler

Solutions & Grid Optimization

Rather than focusing strictly on environmental degradation, the map highlights viable paths forward:

  • Systemic Solutions: Demonstrates the impact of grid modernization, smart microgrids, and high-efficiency transmission lines in preventing energy waste.
  • Individual Action: Shows how localized grid data enables consumers to optimize their household energy footprints, participate in community solar programs, or support regional clean energy initiatives.

The tool ultimately demonstrates that the geographic blueprint and technology required for a transition to clean energy already exist.
